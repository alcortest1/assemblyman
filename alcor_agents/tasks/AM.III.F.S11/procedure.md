## Determine the Distance of Lacing

### Step Instructions

1. Measure the wire bundle distance1.
2. Cut the required length of lacing cord2.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Gather the wires to be bundled together. Must be 1” in diameter or less. Pull the slack out of the wires and use a tape measure to identify the distance.
2. Multiply the distance by 2. Cut that length of lacing cord (e.g., measured distance is 4’. Cut 8’ of lacing cord to use).

## Tie a Starting Knot Using the Single Cord-Lacing Method

### Step Instructions

1. Select the starting position1.
2. Wrap the cord2.
3. Tie a clove hitch3.
4. Secure with a double-looped overhand knot4.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Start the lacing at a clear section of the bundle, free of obstacles.
2. Pass about 8” of cord behind the bundle. Pull the cord from both sides, making a V around the bundle.
3. Pass the 8” strand all the way around the bundle once. Cross the short strand over the long strand and under the loop made. Tightly cinch the hitch to the bundle. Keep tension on the ends to avoid the knot from slipping.
4. Cross the short cord over the long cord four times back towards the clove hitch. Pull both ends until taut. Clip excess tail cord off.

## Tie a Hitch Along the Bundle

### Step Instructions

1. Space the hitch1.
2. Tie a hitch2.
3. Pull the run of the lacing cord to the next hitch.
4. Repeat for as many hitches needed.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Maintain equal distance between each hitch for uniformity and security. An approximate 6” gap provides adequate support. Grasp the bundle by wrapping your fingers around it about 1” away from the previous knot. Start the hitch 1” from the end of your hand by pulling the cord taut to that spot.
2. Pass the cord around the bundle. Align the hitch to the previous knot. Cross the end of the cord over and then under the loop made by the cord running down the bundle. Pull the end all the way through until the hitch is tight.

## Tie a Finishing Knnot on a Bundle

### Step Instructions

1. Space to the finishing knot1.
2. Wrap the cord2.
3. Tie a clove hitch3.
4. Secure with a double-looped overhand knot4.
5. Inspect the lacing5.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Keep the spacing of the hitches if possible. If not, provide adequate space to perform the finishing knot.
2. Hold constant tension on the previous hitch. Grab the cord and bundle firmly in one hand, letting the end of the cord hang freely.
3. Loosely loop the end of the cord around the bundle twice. Cross the end of the cord over the run of cord from the previous hitch. Feed the end of the cord under the loop made going towards the previous hitch. Pull the slack out of the cord until tight.
4. Slip the end of the cord over-and-under the taut lacing toward the finishing knot four times. Pull tension on the cord past the finishing knot.
5. Look along the cord lacing looking for misaligned knots, looseness, and long strands.

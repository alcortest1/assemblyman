## Prepare the Damaged Area

### Step Instructions

1. Clean the portion of the blade to be repaired1.
2. Verify the extent of the damage2.
3. Mark the repair area3.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Wipe the damaged area of the propeller blade with a rag dampened with solvent to remove oils, dirt, and debris.
2. Use a steel scale to determine the length and width of the damage. Use a depth gauge to determine the depth of the damage. Place the depth gauge on a flat surface of the blade. Set the needle to zero by loosening the dial clamping screw and rotating the needle on zero. Center the gauge over the deepest part of the damage, extend the plunger and read the dial. Each line on the ring of the dial is in increments of 0.001”. The small dial is in increments of 0.1” (e.g., the needle points to the number 54 line and the small dial points to 2. This is 0.254”).
3. Outline the repair area with dye or a marker. Draw a contoured shape that tapers into the damage.

## Blend the Damage Out

### Step Instructions

1. File the rough edges of the damage down1.
2. Contour the repair2.
3. Taper the repair into the blade shape3.
4. Fine sand the repair4.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Use a riffler or jeweler file around the perimeter of the damage. File the metal in the forward motion, running the length of the file across it. Edges should feel flush with no burrs or raised points.
2. Use a riffler or jeweler file to round the repair in all directions.
3. File the edges into a smooth transition to the undamaged portions of the blade. No sharp shapes.
4. Progress to a #00 grit sandpaper, leveling the repair. Feel for raised textures.

## Clean the Repair

### Step Instructions

1. Wipe the repair area clean1.
2. Measure the dimensions of the repair2.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Clear all fine metallic dust from the blade surface with a rag dampened with solvent.
2. Use a steel scale to measure the length and width of the repair. Use a depth gauge to measure the depth of the repair.

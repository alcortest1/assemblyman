## AM.I.E.S1 – Perform the Wire Safety on Bolts (By Hand)

> Converted from the source .docx skill sheet. Trailing digits in step text (e.g. `ends2-3`) are cross-references to the numbered **Senior Mechanic Notes** in the same section, and are preserved verbatim.

### Step Instructions

1. Thread the wire into the bolt1.
2. Perform wire wraps2.
3. Pigtail the wire3.
4. Inspect the safety4.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Feed one end of the wire into the starting bolt hole. Pull the wire through until both strands are the same length. Straighten the wire out of both sides of the bolt. Grab each strand and rotate them as if using the wire to tighten the bolt, this determines the right direction to start. Thread one wire tightly along the bolt head toward the other, going under it making a 90° angle with the wires at the bolt hole.
2. Grab a wire in each hand. Use an extended index finger to press on the wire while the other finger grips the wire tightly. Keep the wires in a 90° angle where they are wrapping, this will give 6-8 wraps per inch. Pull all the slack out of the wire and move your hands in a counter clockwise circle without letting your hands touch. Every time your hand is on the left, hold tension on the wire and let go of the wire on the right. While still holding tension, grab the wire on the left with your left hand and right with your right hand. Repeat for the distance to the bolt hole.
3. The wire that is wrapping over the top of the other goes into the next bolt hole. Pull the outside wire around the base of the bolt tightly. Perform the wrapping process in the opposite direction. Make about 12 wraps out the last hole. Using duck bill pliers, grab the last 2 wraps firmly. Pull tension on the wire straight out of the bolt hole and roll it in one big loop (gator roll). Cut the remaining wire leaving 6-8 wraps. Using needle nose pliers, grab the second wrap from the end and curl it back directly at the bolt head. Keep it parallel to the surface.
4. Poke a piece of safety wire between the bolt head and the wire that wraps around it, if it slides between, the safety is too loose. Tug up on the wire going around the bolt head to ensure it doesn’t go over the bolt head. Measure an inch of wraps and verify there are 6-8 wraps. Strum the safety for tautness, if it can move it has too many wraps. Ensure the pigtail has defined wraps and is not a sharp hazard.

## AM.I.E.S1 – Perform the Wire Safety on Bolts (With Safety Wire Pliers)

> Converted from the source .docx skill sheet. Trailing digits in step text (e.g. `ends2-3`) are cross-references to the numbered **Senior Mechanic Notes** in the same section, and are preserved verbatim.

### Step Instructions

1. Thread the wire into the bolt1.
2. Measure the distance to safety2.
3. Perform wire wraps3.
4. Pigtail the wire4.
5. Inspect the safety5.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Feed one end of the wire into the starting bolt hole. Pull the wire through until both strands are the same length. Straighten the wire out of both sides of the bolt. Grab each strand and rotate them as if using the wire to tighten the bolt, this determines the right direction to start. Thread one wire along the bolt head toward the other.
2. Pull both wires to the next bolt hole. Close the plier jaws on the wire at that location and lock them.
3. Pull the twist knob from the bottom of the pliers all the way out. Let go of the plier handles while holding the twist knob, the wire will twist. Release the twist knob into the pliers and repeat until there are 6-8 wraps per inch. Pull the slack out of the wire straight out the bolt hole and perform a gator roll. This will put an extra wrap at the bolt hole the safety came out of, tightening it more. Release the plier jaws.
4. The wire that is wrapping over the top of the other goes into the next bolt hole. Pull the outside wire around the base of the bolt tightly. Clamp the pliers on the wire about 3” from the bolt hole. Rotate the whole plier in a counter clockwise circle to perform the pigtail wraps. Cut the remaining wire leaving 6-8 wraps. Using the plier jaws, grab the second wrap from the end and curl it back directly at the bolt head. Keep it parallel to the surface.
5. Poke a piece of safety wire between the bolt head and the wire that wraps around it, if it slides between, the safety is too loose. Tug up on the wire going around the bolt head to ensure it doesn’t go over the bolt head. Measure an inch of wraps and verify there are 6-8 wraps. Strum the safety for tautness, if it can move it has too many wraps. Ensure the pigtail has defined wraps and is not a sharp hazard.

## AM.I.E.S1 – Perform the Wire Safety on a Turnbuckle (By Hand)

> Converted from the source .docx skill sheet. Trailing digits in step text (e.g. `ends2-3`) are cross-references to the numbered **Senior Mechanic Notes** in the same section, and are preserved verbatim.

### Step Instructions

1. Cut two pieces of safety wire1.
2. Insert and position the wires2.
3. Perform the wraps on the ends of the wires3.
4. Inspect the safety4.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Cut two pieces of wire 30” in length.
2. Insert one wire through the turnbuckle into two even strands. Pull one strand along the turnbuckle toward the cable end, keeping it streamlined. Insert it in the safety hole in one direction. Repeat for the other strand in the opposite direction. Use the second piece of wire and mirror the first wire’s position in the opposite direction.
3. On one end, pull both strands back towards the turnbuckle. Hold the wires taut while using one to wrap around the other three. Use your thumbs to guide the wire into tight coils of 4 wraps. Grab the end of the wrapping wire with duck bill pliers and pull tension up and back toward the completed wraps to cinch them together. Cut the excess wire leaving 4 wraps. Open the duck bill plier jaws wide, using the smooth surface in them, squeeze and roll the wraps for uniformity. Avoid tooling marks on the wraps. Wrap the second wire in the opposite direction. Repeat on the other side.
4. A streamlined silhouette of the safety against the turnbuckle. 4 wraps per wire. Tight coiled wraps with no gaps. Curled wire ends to avoid a sharp hazard.

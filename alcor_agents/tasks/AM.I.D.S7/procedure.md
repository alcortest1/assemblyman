## Determine the Distance and Hose

### Step Instructions

1. Measure the route that the hose needs to take1.
2. Choose a hose and fitting assembly2.
3. Mark the hose3.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Use a tape measure to map the distance. Flexible hoses need room for expansion when they are pressurized. Add 5-8% of the total length needed for the final dimension (e.g., measured distance is 24”. 5-8% of 24” is 1.2”-1.92”. Add them together 24” + 1.9” = 25.9”).
2. The hose is marked on its lay line with a dash number. Each part of the fitting assembly is marked with a corresponding dash number (e.g., on the hose a -6 is printed. On the nipple, B-nut, and collar, a -6 is also printed).
3. Transfer the measurement needed onto the hose with masking tape, wrapped evenly around the hose.

## Cut The Hose

### Step Instructions

1. Secure the hose into a table vise1.
2. Setup a pneumatic cut off wheel2.
3. Cut the hose to length3.
4. Verify the cuts4.

### Senior Mechanic Notes

1. Place the hose horizontally through the vise jaws. Protrude the taped location to the edge of the vise. Close the vise jaws snug against the hose without putting significant tension on it.
2. Connect a composite blade cut off wheel to a compressed air source.
3. Hold the cut off tool with both hands, using your thumb to control the trigger. Start the cut off wheel just before contacting the hose. Slowly lower the cut off wheel, allowing the tool to do the cutting. Keep the blade as vertical as possible to create a clean cut. The tool should make a consistent whining noise as it cuts through the hose. If the noise changes pitch (lower or higher) it indicates how much pressure is being applied to the tool. Lower pitch = too much pressure. Higher pitch = not enough pressure.
4. Unravel the tape from the cut end by pulling it off at a 45° angle towards the end of the hose. Ensure the cut end is as flat as possible.
